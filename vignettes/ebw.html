Energy balancing and independence weights with ebw

The ebw package fits covariate balancing weights for causal inference with either a binary treatment, through energy balancing weights, or a continuous treatment, through independence weights. A single function, energy_balance(), handles both. Every fit records the pieces of its dual representation, so the weight a new unit would receive can be predicted out of sample with predict().

Binary treatment: energy balancing

dat <- sim_confounded_data(n.obs = 300, n.vars = 6, AR.cor = 0.3)
fit_b <- energy_balance(x = dat$x, treatment = dat$trt, estimand = "ATE")
fit_b
#> Energy balancing / independence weights (class 'ebw')
#>   treatment type: binary 
#>   groups (K):     2 
#>   estimand:       ATE 
#>   kernel:         energy 
#>   scaling:        std 
#>   n:              300 
#>   ESS:            191.9  (64.0% of n)
#>   per-group ESS:  0=91.5, 1=100.8 
#>   weight range:   [0, 0.02944]

The fitted weights are returned by weights() as a plain numeric vector, which slots directly into downstream balance tools.

w <- weights(fit_b)
str(w)
#>  num [1:300] 0 0.00437 0.00917 0.01905 0.0056 ...

Out-of-sample prediction

The fit records the quantities that define the weight as an explicit function of the covariates: the fitted weights, the kernel evaluations of a covariate value against the training units, the fixed centering constants, the threshold multiplier, and the ridge parameter. Evaluating that function at a new covariate value gives the weight a genuinely new unit would receive, so predict() returns out-of-sample weights without refitting.

new <- sim_confounded_data(n.obs = 50, n.vars = 6, AR.cor = 0.3)
w_new <- predict(fit_b, newdata = new$x)
summary(w_new)
#>    Min. 1st Qu.  Median    Mean 3rd Qu.    Max. 
#> 0.00000 0.08669 0.59439 0.71049 1.08153 3.15303

Continuous treatment: independence weights

For a continuous treatment, independence_weights() fits weights that drive the weighted joint distribution of covariates and treatment toward independence.

cdat <- sim_continuous_data(n.obs = 300, n.vars = 4, alpha.norm2 = 0.5)
fit_c <- independence_weights(A = cdat$a, X = cdat$x)
fit_c
#> Energy balancing / independence weights (class 'ebw')
#>   treatment type: continuous 
#>   kernel:         energy 
#>   scaling:        none 
#>   n:              300 
#>   ESS:            242.2  (80.7% of n)
#>   weight range:   [0, 3.341]

Prediction is again out of sample, but the balancing function now depends on both the covariates and the treatment value, so both are required.

cnew <- sim_continuous_data(n.obs = 50, n.vars = 4, alpha.norm2 = 0.5)
w_cnew <- predict(fit_c, newdata = list(x = cnew$x, a = cnew$a))
summary(w_cnew)
#>    Min. 1st Qu.  Median    Mean 3rd Qu.    Max. 
#> 0.06183 1.01857 1.19606 1.26732 1.53886 2.90107

The continuous balance report gives per-covariate distance correlations with the treatment, the overall distance covariance, and the weighted versus unweighted marginal means.

Moment constraints

Two optional moment constraints refine the continuous fit. preserve_means forces the weighted mean of every covariate and of the treatment to equal the unweighted sample mean, and decorrelate_moments forces the weighted treatment-covariate covariances to zero. Both are solved with osqp, and the out-of-sample predictor gains a dual term that is linear in the constraint features, so prediction stays out of sample.

fit_pm <- independence_weights(A = cdat$a, X = cdat$x, preserve_means = TRUE)
w_pm <- weights(fit_pm)

# the weighted covariate means now match the unweighted means
round(max(abs(colSums(w_pm * cdat$x) / sum(w_pm) - colMeans(cdat$x))), 12)
#> [1] 0

# and prediction remains available out of sample
head(predict(fit_pm, newdata = list(x = cnew$x, a = cnew$a)))
#> [1] 1.9470756 0.1497086 0.3171436 3.0152929 0.7849770 0.9970677
